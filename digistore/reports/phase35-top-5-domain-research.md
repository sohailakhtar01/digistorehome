## 1. The Self-Sufficient Backyard — score 80/100

**Vendor:** `sbackyard` · $51.80 price · **$27.18 net** · 9% cart conversion ·
**1.28% cancellation**

**1. Product.** A 265-page print book on off-grid homesteading by **Ron and Johanna
Melchiore**, who have lived off-grid since the late 1970s. Named, real, verifiable
authors — the only product in the top five where authorship is not in question.

**2. Best keyword.** `self sufficient backyard` — branded.

**3. Search demand.** The largest measured cluster in the study:

| Keyword | Volume/mo | Competition index |
|---|---|---|
| `self sufficient backyard` | **1,900** | 99 |
| `the self sufficient backyard` | **1,600** | 100 |
| `self sufficient backyard book` | 390 | 97 |
| `self sufficient backyard pdf` | 110 | 32 |
| `self sufficient backyard review` | 90 | 97 |
| **Branded total** | **≈4,090/mo** | |

Non-branded pool it sits inside: `homesteading book` **1,000**,
`best homesteading books` 390, `self sufficiency book` 320,
`off grid living book` 90, `backyard homestead book` 70 — **≈1,870/mo more.**

Competition index of 97–100 on the branded terms means advertisers are actively
bidding. That is independent confirmation the traffic converts.

**4. Bing opportunity.** `NO_VALID_DATA`. On the Google proxy the page is: Reddit
(a 5-year-old thread) #1, Amazon #2, Mother Earth News store #3, Goodreads #4,
`cz.eureka.com` #5 (parasite blog), `vocal.media` #6 (parasite). No authority
publisher, no dedicated review site, and **the Digistore24 funnel does not appear
on page 1 at all** — the affiliate offer is invisible in its own brand SERP.

**5. Why it can work.** Highest measured demand in the queue meets the best refund
profile in the entire 1,381-product dataset. A 1.28% cancellation rate means buyers
keep it — which is what you would expect from a real book by real authors. Two
parasite pages holding page one is direct evidence that nothing well-made competes.

**6. Biggest risk.** **Amazon ranks #2 and sells the book outright.** A reader who
wants it can buy it in one click without touching your link. Any content has to
give people a reason to use the funnel — usually the bundle/bonus structure the
Digistore24 offer carries and the retail edition does not. Secondary: `pdf`
intent at 110/mo is people looking for a free copy.

**7. Confidence: HIGH** on demand and economics · **UNVERIFIED** on Bing.

## 2. The Lost SuperFoods — score 73/100

**Vendor:** `lostrec` · $56.78 price · **$27.31 net** · 19% cart conversion ·
**1.70% cancellation**

**1. Product.** A 270-page survival cookbook — 126 shelf-stable foods, pemmican,
long-term storage methods.

**2. Best keyword.** `long term food storage` — **non-branded**, deliberately.

**3. Search demand.** `the lost superfoods` 210/mo (declining: 320 → 140 over
12 months); `the lost superfoods review` 20/mo. The brand alone is too thin. The
non-branded pool is where this lives: **`long term food storage` 1,300/mo at a
$3.04 CPC**, plus `survival food recipes` 70/mo. That is **6× the branded volume**
and it is buyer traffic — a $3.04 CPC is not paid for browsers.

**4. Bing opportunity.** `NO_VALID_DATA`. Google proxy: Amazon #1, Reddit #2
(r/preppers, 4 years old), Facebook #3, Goodreads #4, `bulbapp.com` #5 (parasite),
eBay #6, `accessnewswire.com` #7 (press release), Quora #8. **Zero authority
publishers and no official vendor page.**

**5. Why it can work.** 1.70% cancellation is the lowest of any promotable product
measured — buyers keep this book. Combined with 19% cart conversion, the funnel
demonstrably works. The competing pages are a 4-year-old Reddit thread, a Facebook
post and a press release.

**6. Biggest risk.** **Amazon (#1) and eBay (#6) both sell it** — the same channel
problem as #1 but worse, because both marketplaces outrank everything else.
Separately, the credited author "Claude Davis" also fronts Home Doctor from a
different vendor; Phase 3 found a competitor asserting the name is a fictional
frontman. **This is still unverified and must be settled before any content is
written**, because it determines whether the product can be endorsed honestly.

**7. Confidence: HIGH** on economics and SERP structure · **MEDIUM** on the
authorship question · **UNVERIFIED** on Bing.

## 3. Tube Magic — score 71/100

**Vendor:** `tubemagic` · $47.00/month · **$265.11 net (lifetime, not per sale)** ·
**1% cart conversion** · 8.94% cancellation

**1. Product.** AI tooling for YouTube creators — script generation, keyword
research, thumbnail and title optimisation.

**2. Best keyword.** `tube magic` — branded.

**3. Search demand.** **1,600/mo** — the only four-figure single branded term in
the queue. Adjacent: **`youtube automation tools` 170/mo at a $10.16 CPC**, the
highest commercial value measured anywhere in this study, and
`best ai tools for youtube` 30/mo at $6.45.

**4. Bing opportunity.** `NO_VALID_DATA`. Google proxy: Medium #1 (parasite),
Trustpilot #2, YouTube #3, `productinsightai.com` #4, Reddit #5, Nairaland #6,
**the official `tubemagic.com` only at #7**, `outlierkit.com` #8 — and that last
one ranks an **alternatives** page, i.e. a competitor is already capturing this
brand's traffic and redirecting it.

**5. Why it can work.** Real, sustained demand and genuinely expensive adjacent
keywords. The official site sitting at #7 in its own brand SERP is unusual and
exploitable.

**6. Biggest risk — and it is the largest in this five.** **1% cart conversion is
the worst in the queue.** Phase 2 established the $265.11 "net" is lifetime value
on a $47/month subscription, not a payout per sale — the real front-end commission
is roughly **$23**. Trustpilot rates it **2.6 "Poor"** and the #1 Medium review
reports ~2.8/5. Recommending it warmly would not be honest; reviewing it accurately
would suppress the conversion rate further. **Of the five, this is the one I would
fund last.**

**7. Confidence: HIGH** on demand · **LOW** on economics as advertised ·
**UNVERIFIED** on Bing.

## 4. David's Shield — score 63/100

**Vendor:** `blackoutusa` · $83.23 price · **$44.71 net** · 20% cart conversion ·
9.89% cancellation

**1. Product.** "BlastProof: David's Shield" — a faith-framed EMP / grid-down
preparedness guide combining Faraday-shielding basics with Amish-inspired
self-reliance methods. $67 digital edition.

**2. Best keyword.** `davids shield` — branded.

**3. Search demand.** **1,900/mo measured** — but see the risk below, because an
unknown share of it is not this product. Qualifiers `davids shield review`,
`davids shield generator` and `davids shield blackout` all return **no data**.
Non-branded is thin: `emp survival book` 50/mo, `how to prepare for emp` 50/mo.

**4. Bing opportunity.** `NO_VALID_DATA`. Google proxy — **the weakest competitor
set measured in the entire screen**: Amazon #1, Yahoo Finance press release #2,
`davidsshieldfoundation.org` #3 (an unrelated protection-training charity),
Google Books #4, `davidshields.com` #5 (an unrelated bestselling author),
**`scribehow.com` #6, `open.firstory.me` #7 and `sites.google.com` #8 — three
parasite pages in a row** — and `newpelican.com` #9, a local newspaper running
affiliate content. Not one dedicated review site holds any position.

**5. Why it can work.** 20% cart conversion is the joint-highest of the five, at
$44.71 per sale. Three consecutive parasite pages on page one is the clearest
signal in this dataset that no competent competitor has bothered.

**6. Biggest risk.** **The 1,900/mo is contaminated.** Two unrelated entities —
David's Shield Foundation and the author David Shields — rank on page one, so the
addressable share of that volume is unknown and could be a small fraction.
This is the reason it carries the ambiguous-name penalty and ranks 4th rather than
1st. Resolving it needs a clickstream or intent breakdown that is not available
here. Amazon also sells the guide at #1.

**7. Confidence: MEDIUM** — high on SERP weakness, genuinely uncertain on how much
of the demand is reachable · **UNVERIFIED** on Bing.

## 5. Home Doctor — score 62/100

**Vendor:** `homedoctor` · $49.65 price · **$26.83 net** · 12% cart conversion ·
**2.81% cancellation**

**1. Product.** *Home Doctor — Practical Medicine for Every Household*, a 304-page
book on managing common medical problems with household supplies.

**2. Best keyword.** `home remedies book` — **non-branded**.

**3. Search demand.** Branded is small: `home doctor book` 90/mo,
`home doctor book review` 10/mo. The non-branded pool is the opportunity:
**`home remedies book` 1,300/mo** and **`survival medicine book` 390/mo** —
roughly **17× the branded terms**.

**4. Bing opportunity.** `NO_VALID_DATA`. Google proxy: Amazon #1, Goodreads #2,
Quora #3, `vocal.media` #4 (parasite), **Scribd #5 (a full copy of the document)**,
Medium #6 (parasite, 4 years old), `ideasbeat.com` #7 (small affiliate site),
Etsy #8. No authority publisher anywhere on the page.

**5. Why it can work.** A large, durable non-branded market that a genuinely useful
resource site can serve, plus 2.81% cancellation showing buyers are satisfied. The
competing set is two marketplaces, a Q&A page and three parasite posts.

**6. Biggest risk.** **Scribd hosts the document at #5** — a meaningful share of
searchers are looking for a free copy, not a purchase. Amazon and Etsy both sell
the print edition. And this is health-adjacent content: "practical medicine for
every household" needs careful, non-prescriptive framing, though it is far less
exposed than a supplement claim.

**7. Confidence: MEDIUM** · **UNVERIFIED** on Bing.

## Why the Phase 3 top five changed

| Product | Phase 3 | Phase 3.5 | What changed |

Amazon, Mother Earth News and Countryside all sell near-identical seed kits. Phase 3 saw none of this because it looked only at a Bing SERP that is now withdrawn. |

| **David's Shield** | not ranked | **#4 (63)** | Never SERP-tested in Phase 3. Carries 1,900/mo and the weakest competitor set found. |
