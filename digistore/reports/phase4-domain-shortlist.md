## Two names that need inspection before purchase

**`youtubeautomationtools.com`** is an exact match for a 170/mo term at a **$10.16
CPC** — the highest-value keyword measured in this entire study — and it is
available. But it carries **one archived snapshot from 2025**, meaning it was
registered and dropped within roughly the last year. Recently-dropped domains can
carry spam penalties. Inspect that snapshot before buying; if it looks like a
churn-and-burn affiliate page, take `youtubetoolreport.com` instead, which is
clean. That is why the table above recommends the clean name.

**`herbalremedylibrary.com`** ran a real site (25–46 KB pages) from Nov 2024 to
Feb 2025, then returned HTTP 402 in Mar 2025 and lapsed. It may carry usable
backlinks — or a bad history. Worth inspecting, not worth buying blind.

## The largest term in the study has no clean product

`herbal remedies book` is **5,400/mo at $1.16 CPC** — larger than every other
keyword measured, branded or not. Available names include
**`theherbalremedybook.com`** and **`homeherbalguide.com`**, both clean.

**But its natural Digistore24 product is the Medicinal Garden Kit, which Phase 3.5
disqualified**: McGill University's science unit ranks #2 on its brand SERP
debunking it, and the vendor's own page claims the seeds are "FDA approved", which
is not something the FDA does. So this cluster is the biggest traffic opportunity
in the dataset **and currently has no product I can recommend promoting honestly.**
Registering the name is cheap and defensible; building on it needs a different
offer first.

## Verification items I closed this phase

**Sales pages — partially opened.**
- `theselfsufficientbackyard.com` is **the authors' own site**, not the Digistore24
  funnel. It confirms Ron and Johanna are real people with a 40-year homesteading
  history. Claims are modest and non-medical: *"Everything they've used to live a
  good life for the past 40 years was either made by them or paid for with what the
  homestead produced."* **Low compliance risk — this is the cleanest product of the
  five to write about honestly.**
- `tubemagic.com`: **30-day money-back guarantee, no free trial** — *"We have
  disabled free trials for now due to spam."* Claims "200K+ Scripts Generated". The
  disabled trial is a mild negative signal for a tool in a spam-heavy niche.
- `thesuperfoodslost.com` does not resolve (DNS failure). The Lost SuperFoods and
  Home Doctor funnel pages remain unopened.

**"Claude Davis" authorship — UNRESOLVED, and I could not settle it either way.**
He has an Amazon author page, a Goodreads author record, and named co-authors
(Art Rude, Lex Rooker, Fred Dwight) across *The Lost SuperFoods*. Biographical
detail is published but unverifiable. **I found no independent evidence that the
name is fabricated, and no independent verification of the identity either.**
Practical consequence: write about the book's *contents*, and make no claims about
the author's biography. That keeps content honest regardless of which is true.

**Still open:** the Bing gap. No Bing SERP has been validated for any of these
clusters — `data/serp/phase35-bing-fault.md`. Three manual lookups would close it.
