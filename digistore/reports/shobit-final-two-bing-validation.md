**A click sent to Medicinal Garden Kit is worth 10.7x a click sent to Lost
Frontier Handbook.** Lost Frontier would have to win roughly *ten times the
traffic*, on the deeper-indexed of the two SERPs, merely to break even against
it. Nothing in the SERP comparison below comes close to a 10x swing.

Lost Frontier's 3.0% cart conversion is near the bottom of the entire
150-product shortlist. That is not a blemish on an otherwise good offer — it is
the offer reporting that people who reach the sales page do not buy.

**And Bing tells us why, in its own Related Searches:**

> `the lost frontier handbook scam` · **`the lost frontier handbook pdf`** · `the lost frontier book`

`pdf` is free-download intent. It is a $37 digital book, and one of the three
queries Bing volunteers for it is people looking for a free copy. The 3% cart
conversion and the piracy-intent related search corroborate each other. **This
leak cannot be fixed by better content, and it does not exist for a physical
product like a seed kit.**

## 2. Two findings that apply to both

**The portfolio giants are absent.** `consumerhealthdigest.com` (23 of 30 proxy
SERPs), `supplementmag.com` (19 of 30) and `dailyhealthsupplement.com` (18 of 30)
appear in **neither** of these SERPs. Those incumbents live in the *supplement*
vertical. Both of these products sit outside it, and both SERPs are contested
instead by small, weak, single-topic sites. **This materially improves small-site
odds for both candidates**, and it is a new finding from this data.

**A fourth consecutive dataset with zero EMDs ranking.** Twenty ranking domains
across these two SERPs; not one contains the product brand. Combined with the
30-SERP measurement (EMD average position 5.0 vs 3.9 for non-EMDs, only 1 of 11
at #1), the exact-match-domain tactic from the original hypothesis is now
comprehensively refuted.

### Where each loses ground

**Medicinal Garden Kit — the above-the-fold problem.** AI answer, carousel,
result 1, a 3-video block, a 6-item short-video block, *then* results 2–10.
Position 1 sits above all of it; positions 3–5 sit a long way down. This is a
real CTR cost that the shallow index conceals, and it means the target is the top
two slots, not "somewhere on page 1." Mitigating it: the videos themselves are
weak — 602, 278 and 891 views.

**Medicinal Garden Kit — one strong competitor.** `shelfinsider.com` at #3 is a
registered dietitian who grew all ten plants and evaluated them herb by herb.
That is genuinely hard to beat, and we should not pretend otherwise.

**Lost Frontier Handbook — an actively churning SERP.** Nine of ten pages are
under nine months old. Entry is easy; retention is not. Every quarter would mean
defending against new pages, on a query worth 76 cents a click.

## 4. Why the credibility problem here is NOT the Amino problem

You are right to expect the same axe. It does not fall the same way, and the
distinction is the point:

**Advanced Amino Formula:** the *entire selling proposition* was one physician's
credibility, and that physician surrendered a licence under Board findings of
"dishonesty and corruption." The honest review had to destroy the reason to buy.
No survivable framing existed.

**Medicinal Garden Kit:** the selling proposition is *"grow ten medicinal herbs
at home."* The seeds are real, heirloom and non-GMO, and Bing's own synthesis of
user reviews reports successful germination across the set. The defects are
(a) a false "FDA approved" trust badge — the FDA does not approve seeds — and
(b) McGill's dissent on herbal efficacy. An honest review can say: *you receive
real heirloom seeds and a usable guide; ignore the "FDA approved" line, it is not
a thing; the medicinal uses are traditional, not clinically proven.* **That
review is honest, differentiated, and still recommends the product to the right
buyer** — a gardener who wants heirloom herb seeds and a guide. Amino had no
such version.

**Lost Frontier Handbook's credibility is cleaner still.** Suzanne Sherman is
independently verifiable (Red Hot Chilly Prepper Podcast, The Wasatch Report,
Survivopedia, PrepperCon), and Bing's own AI answer independently describes her
as a former attorney homesteading in Utah. Nothing has to be contradicted. That
is why it is runner-up rather than rejected: **it wins on honesty and loses on
money.**

## 5. THE OPEN GATE — read before spending anything

**Nicole Apelian has not been investigated at the depth that killed Advanced
Amino Formula.** She is verifiable at surface level — PhD, herbalist, History
Channel *Alone* participant, author. I have *not* run the equivalent of the
medical-board search that ended the Amino candidacy. Marked UNKNOWN, not clean.

**This matters more than usual here**, because Bing's own Related Searches show
her name *is* the demand:

> `nicole apelian garden kit reviews` · `medicinal garden kit nicole apelian` · `nicole apelian garden kit`

**Three of the eight related searches carry her name.** That is precisely the
structural exposure Amino had: if the named authority has a problem, the offer
falls the same way. Declaring a winner and then buying a domain without running
that check would repeat the Amino mistake in reverse.

One point in the runner-up's favour: Lost Frontier's related searches contain
**no author name at all**, so it is structurally less exposed to author risk.
