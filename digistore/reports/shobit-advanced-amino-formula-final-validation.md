## 1. The credibility gate — VERIFIED, AND IT FAILS

In Phase 5 I wrote: *"If confirmed, the vendor's credibility — the entire basis
for choosing it — collapses."* I have now confirmed it.

The Trustpilot allegation I refused to treat as fact was **substantially
accurate**. Two independent sources, including the California Medical Board case
document:

| Date | Jurisdiction | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **1994** | **Medical Board of California** | Findings of **"gross incompetence"**, **"repeated acts of gross negligence"**, and **"acts of dishonesty and corruption which are substantially related to the qualifications, functions, or duties of a physician and surgeon"**. Rather than contest, **he surrendered his California medical licence.** |
| **1995** | Nevada Board of Medical Examiners | Publicly admonished for **failing to notify Nevada of the California surrender**; $300 fine and public reprimand. |
| **2006–07** | Nevada Board of Medical Examiners | Charged with **substandard care of a 76-year-old patient**. Settled 2007: reprimand, **$5,000 fine**, $6,457.57 costs, 16 hours CME on cancer screening. |

**In fairness, and this matters:** Dr Shallenberger **currently holds an active
Nevada medical licence** (#4951, expiring 2027) and practises in Carson City. He
is a real, currently-licensed physician. The 1994 action is 32 years old. The
product itself — amino acids — is benign, and the company is genuinely
established with retail presence on Amazon and Walmart.

### Why this is still fatal *for this specific play*

Advanced Amino Formula was chosen over 1,380 other products for exactly one
reason: it was the only offer where **a real, credible, named physician** anchored
the marketing, in a dataset otherwise full of fabricated VSL doctors. The vendor
sells on that authority — *"Supplements Formulated By Doctors"*,
*"Dr. Frank Shallenberger's Favorite Supplements"*.

That differentiator is gone. What remains is a fork with no good branch:

- **Write the honest review** — disclosing a surrendered licence under findings
  that used the words *"dishonesty and corruption"*, plus a later reprimand for
  substandard care — and the page will not convert. It is a page that talks
  readers out of buying.
- **Omit it** while trading on his medical authority — and we are doing precisely
  what I rejected TonicGreens, iGenics and Pineal Guardian for. The whole point
  of this screen was not to become the thing we filtered out.

I rejected **iGenics** because its VSL doctor could not be verified. Here the
doctor *can* be verified, and verification is the problem. Consistency requires
the same answer.

### Collateral: the cluster falls with it

Five of the final 15 came from this vendor. All inherit the same disclosure
problem:

`Advanced Amino Formula` · `Advanced Mitochondrial Formula` ·
`Advanced Memory Formula` · `Pep Tonic` · `Advanced Collagen Plus`

The horizontal-scaling advantage that made this pick attractive is now a
concentration risk pointing the wrong way.

**Could we write a truthful review without repeating unsupported claims?**
About the *product*, yes. About the *doctor* who is the brand's entire selling
proposition, only by disclosing the disciplinary record — which defeats the page.
