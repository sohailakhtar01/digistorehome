# Where the money actually is — 2026-08-26

All keyword and SERP data from DataForSEO, US desktop, captured today. Raw JSON
in `raw/dfs/`. No figure here is estimated.

---

## 1. The economics, stated plainly

**Cost:** $23 Claude Code + $10 DataForSEO = **$33/month**, plus a one-time $10
domain.

**Revenue per sale:** $45.56 net (Digistore24, confirmed in the account).

**Break-even is less than one sale a month.** That is a genuinely low bar and
this site should clear it.

The harder question is what "good money" requires. Working from the vendor's own
stated EPC of $2.75 per click to their sales page:

```
$45.56 / $2.75  =  ~17 clicks to the offer per sale
```

At a realistic 8% of review-page readers clicking the affiliate link, one sale
needs roughly **210 review-page visits**.

**Where those visits can come from:**

| Source | Realistic monthly, 6–12 months out | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Branded review terms | 60–100 | The entire branded cluster is 190/mo |
| Guide referrals via KitCallout | 40–80 | ~1% of guide traffic clicks through |
| **Total to the review page** | **~100–180** | |

That is roughly **0.5–1 sale a month** to start, rising as the guides age into
the spring season. Covers costs. Does not yet pay for anyone's time.

**The constraint is market size, not execution.** The site is built well and the
clusters it targets are winnable. There are simply only 190 branded searches a
month for this product, and the whole non-branded category — survival seed
vaults, heirloom seed kits, everything — adds only about 1,200 more.

To be specific about the ceiling: dominating *every* commercial term in this
category, branded and not, tops out around **$100–150/month**. That is the
honest number.

## 2. Six products tested today. Five failed for one reason.

| Product | Head term | KD | RD | SERP verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Forgotten Home Apothecary | 22,200 | 0 | 1 | **REJECT** — Walmart, eBay, Etsy, Amazon + shopping carousel |
| Harvest Right freeze dryer | 49,500 | 0–20 | 0–96 | **REJECT** — harvestright.com holds positions 1, 2, 3 *and* 4 |
| Lost Book of Herbal Remedies | 3,600 | 4 | 5 | **REJECT** — eBay, Walmart, Amazon + carousel. Vox at #6 |
| Survival seed vault | 110–260 | 0–2 | 1–4 | **REJECT** — the sellers own it, plus a carousel |
| My Patriot Supply reviews | 170 | 0–12 | 0–2 | Open, but 170/mo is not a business |
| Berkey water filter review | 590 | 0 | 5–11 | Small, and the brand has had regulatory trouble |

### The pattern

**If a product is sold on Amazon, Walmart, Etsy or eBay, Google fills the SERP
with those shops and an affiliate review cannot rank.** Low KD and low RD are
meaningless there — retail category pages carry almost no page-level links,
which is exactly why the difficulty scores look inviting. That mirage has now
appeared three separate times in one day's research.

**The Medicinal Garden Kit is winnable precisely because it has no retail
distribution.** It is sold only through `medicinalseedkit.com` via Digistore24.
No Amazon listing means no Amazon result, which leaves the SERP to affiliates
and spam — and the incumbent affiliates are one page at position 15.

### The filter for product number two

Before researching any further product, check in this order:

1. **Is it sold on Amazon or Walmart?** If yes, stop. Nothing else matters.
2. **Does the brand's own site occupy the top 3–4 results?** If yes, stop.
3. Only then look at volume, KD and referring domains.

That single filter would have saved five of today's six investigations.

## 3. What actually moves the number

Ranked by leverage per hour spent.

### 3.1 Email capture — the highest-leverage free move

This niche is violently seasonal. `coneflower seeds` runs at 9,900 in December
and **49,500 in April**. The guides will attract their audience through autumn
and winter, which is precisely when nobody is buying.

Without a list, that traffic bounces and is gone. With a list, an audience
accumulated over six quiet months can be converted in a single February email,
when demand is climbing and people are actually ordering seed.

A lead magnet that fits the content honestly:

- A **cold stratification calendar** — which of the ten species to start when,
  counting backwards from the reader's own last frost date.
- A **seed-starting schedule** for the ten kit species.

Both are genuinely useful, both are already 80% written inside existing guides,
and both are wanted most by exactly the person who will buy seed in spring.

This is the single biggest gap between what the site is now and what a
professional affiliate operation looks like.

### 3.2 Seasonal CTAs

The KitCallout is currently the same in August and February. It should not be.
In autumn the honest pitch is "order now so the seed is in the fridge by
January"; in March it is "there is still time for the easy five". Timing the
copy to where the reader actually is in their season is free and converts
better than any amount of persuasion.

### 3.3 Product two, found with the filter above

The economics only change materially with a second money page. But it has to
clear the retail filter, and none of today's candidates did. Worth doing
properly rather than fast.

## 4. What not to do

**Do not widen into unrelated low-difficulty topics.** Composting and
gluten-free bread pass a KD filter and fail the only test that matters: nobody
arriving from them will buy a medicinal seed kit. That is how a review site
turns into a content farm.

**Do not buy links or publish thin pages to hit a page count.** The one thing
this site currently has that competitors on that SERP do not is that it is
honest and specific. That is also the only durable advantage available at this
budget.

**Do not expect the branded review page to carry the business.** It is a
conversion asset serving a 190/mo cluster. The guides are the traffic engine and
the list is what turns traffic into repeatable revenue.

---

## 5. Realistic expectation

No ranking can be promised, and none is here.

If the guides rank as the data suggests they can — RD 0–5 clusters against
independent blogs — a reasonable outcome by spring 2027 is a few thousand
monthly visits and **one to three sales a month**. That covers the $33 and
leaves a small surplus.

Turning that into real income requires either a second product that clears the
retail filter, or an email list that lets one season's accumulated audience be
converted at the moment demand peaks. Probably both.

The build quality is not the bottleneck. It was never going to be.
